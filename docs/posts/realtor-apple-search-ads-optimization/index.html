Executive summary

The project changed the unit of work from “adjust a bid” to “run a validated decision process.”

Realtor.com’s Apple Search Ads team was managing bids manually. Decisions happened relatively infrequently, the logic was difficult to reproduce, and much of the operational knowledge sat with one specialist. Our team designed a framework that could be reviewed weekly, explained keyword by keyword, exported for execution, and improved using evidence from historical outcomes.

3,053keywords analyzed in the final framework
88keywords with execution-ready recommendations
$85.7Kmonthly spend represented by the final recommendation pool

01 · Business problem

Manual bid management limited speed, consistency, and trust.

Slow reaction

Bid changes happened only around 58 times per year, making it difficult to respond quickly to performance and competitive shifts.

Key-person dependency

Decision logic was concentrated in individual expertise, which made handoff and scale difficult.

Low transparency

Spreadsheet-based work made it hard to see why a keyword received a recommendation or whether a prior decision worked.

No formal validation loop

The team lacked a consistent process for comparing the framework’s recommended action with the outcome of real bid changes.

Design objective: build a weekly bid decision support system that is faster to review, easier to audit, and grounded in business evidence.

02 · Data foundation

Start with a reliable keyword pool before making recommendations.

The framework combined Apple Search Ads keyword performance, AppsFlyer revenue and lead attribution, Apple impression share data, keyword/campaign/ad-group lookups, and historical bid change records. The key design choice was to separate keywords by evidence sufficiency rather than forcing every keyword into an action.

Actionableenough performance data to enter the bid decision pipeline
Monitoringsome activity, but not enough revenue or volume for a confident action
Dormantinsufficient activity or zero taps; no bid action recommended

03 · Framework architecture

Two lenses, category-specific rules, one final action.

Each keyword was classified across two dimensions. Brand Segment captured the role of the keyword in the competitive landscape; Funnel Stage captured the user’s intent. Different combinations were evaluated with different metrics, so the framework did not treat every keyword as if it had the same business job.

01 · IngestApple Search Ads, AppsFlyer, impression share, and bid history
02 · ClassifyBrand / competitor / non-brand × awareness / consideration / conversion
03 · EvaluateROAS, CPI, CTR, CVR, lead rate, or share of voice
04 · VoteCombine brand and funnel signals into one direction
05 · SizeUse signal strength to assign ±10% or ±20%
06 · ExportReview in the dashboard and prepare the action file

Examples of category logic: Brand keywords were evaluated primarily through Share of Voice; Competitor keywords balanced ROAS and CPI; Awareness keywords emphasized CTR and CPI; Consideration keywords emphasized ROAS and CVR; Conversion keywords emphasized ROAS and lead rate.

04 · Recommendation output

The framework converted signals into a decision with direction, magnitude, and confidence.

The final output did not stop at “increase” or “decrease.” It assigned a recommendation magnitude and confidence level, giving the strategist a way to prioritize high-conviction actions and inspect conflicts before execution.

+20%strong increase signal · 13 keywords · $669 monthly spend
+10%moderate increase signal · 19 keywords · $38,768 monthly spend
-10% / -20%decrease signals · 56 keywords · approximately $46K monthly spend
42high-confidence actions where both lenses agreed
46medium-confidence actions with a one-sided signal
16×ROAS difference between decrease and increase pools

06 · Backtest and validation

The backtest showed where the framework was strong and where it needed a guardrail.

We applied the framework to 59 real bid-change events from January to May 2026 using point-in-time metrics available at each event. We compared the framework recommendation with the actual company action and evaluated 14-day before/after changes in profit and ROAS.

Decrease logic was strongly validated.

Among the 17 events with sufficient validation data where both the company and framework recommended Decrease, profit improved 88% of the time. Median profit improvement was +$5,018 and median ROAS improvement was +0.694.

The framework caught risky increases.

In all three observed cases where the company increased while the framework recommended Decrease, profit worsened. This made the decrease logic the strongest validated part of the system.

Guardrail discovered: high ROAS does not automatically justify a Brand keyword increase. When Share of Voice is already high, saturation can matter more than funnel signals.

07 · Business impact and handoff

A portfolio solution, not a one-time analysis.

Operational

Replaced manual spreadsheet review with a repeatable weekly workflow and export-ready action list.

Financial

Identified approximately $46K per month in the decrease pool and a 16× ROAS difference between decrease and increase pools.

Governance

Documented the rules, confidence levels, triggered logic, and backtest evidence so recommendations could be audited.

Next iteration

Strengthen Brand Share of Voice logic by lowering the decrease threshold or blocking Brand increases above a saturation threshold.
